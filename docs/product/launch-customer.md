# Launch Customer

**Status:** Research hypothesis

**Last updated:** 2026-09-13

## Decision

Stagenum's initial launch customer is a small residential general contractor or
remodeler who manages multi-stage projects directly with homeowners and collects
payment at defined milestones.

This is a market-entry focus, not a permanent product boundary. Stagenum retains
a profession-neutral foundation so it can later expand to specialty trades,
installers, and remote independent professionals when evidence supports doing so.

## Primary user

The primary user is the person responsible for keeping a residential remodeling
project moving from completed work to client approval and payment.

In the initial segment, that person is commonly:

- the business owner;
- an owner-operator;
- a general contractor acting as project manager; or
- an office or project coordinator working closely with the owner.

The user may perform work personally or coordinate employees and subcontractors.
What matters is that they own the client-facing milestone, documentation,
approval, and billing workflow.

## Buyer

The likely buyer is the business owner or operator who feels the financial and
administrative cost of delayed approvals, disputed completion, and slow milestone
payments.

The homeowner is a required participant and beneficiary, but is not expected to
purchase Stagenum.

## Ideal customer profile

The strongest initial fit is a residential contractor or remodeler that:

- completes projects over multiple days or phases;
- bills by deposit, progress milestone, deliverable, or completion stage;
- works directly with the homeowner or property owner;
- can define observable stage outcomes or acceptance criteria;
- regularly captures job-site photos, notes, files, or checklists;
- currently coordinates approvals through text, email, phone calls, or informal
  walkthroughs;
- prepares invoices separately from the evidence and approval conversation;
- uses a phone as an essential job-site tool;
- has enough simultaneous or recurring project work for process consistency to
  matter; and
- can adopt a focused tool without an enterprise implementation.

## Best initial project types

Stagenum should first be tested on projects with visible, client-understandable
stages, such as:

- kitchen or bathroom remodeling;
- basement finishing;
- room additions and substantial renovations;
- decks, patios, and exterior projects;
- flooring or finish work delivered across several stages; and
- other residential improvements with milestone-based billing.

These examples are hypotheses for recruitment and testing, not product-specific
templates promised for the MVP.

## Poor initial fit

Stagenum is not initially optimized for:

- single-visit or low-value service calls;
- businesses paid entirely at the point of service;
- large commercial or public construction projects;
- projects requiring complex retainage, pay applications, lien-waiver exchange,
  certified payroll, or multi-tier approval chains;
- subcontractors whose only client is a general contractor and whose billing
  workflow is controlled by that contractor's enterprise system;
- businesses seeking full estimating, scheduling, dispatch, crew management,
  procurement, or accounting software; or
- providers whose work cannot be divided into meaningful reviewable stages.

These customers may become relevant later, but including their specialized
requirements now would distort the core MVP.

## Current workflow hypothesis

The target provider commonly:

1. agrees on scope and payment milestones through a proposal, contract, estimate,
   or conversation;
2. documents progress in the phone's camera roll, text messages, email, or a
   field-documentation tool;
3. tells the homeowner that a phase is complete;
4. answers questions or handles corrections through calls and messages;
5. infers approval from a reply, walkthrough, or lack of objection;
6. creates an invoice in a separate accounting or field-service tool; and
7. follows up again when approval or payment status is unclear.

The fragmentation—not the absence of any single feature—is the problem Stagenum
intends to solve.

## Core pain hypothesis

The customer is not primarily buying another invoice generator. They are buying
a shorter, clearer path between:

> **Work completed → Evidence understood → Client decision recorded → Invoice
> issued → Payment tracked**

The pain is strongest when:

- milestone payments materially affect cash flow;
- the client was not present while work was performed;
- progress is hidden behind walls or later work;
- several conversations are needed to establish completion;
- a requested correction is confused with added scope;
- the provider delays invoicing while assembling evidence; or
- the parties later disagree about what was shown or accepted.

## Desired outcomes

The provider wants to:

- present completed work professionally;
- give the homeowner confidence without scheduling another walkthrough;
- receive an explicit decision instead of interpreting informal messages;
- distinguish a correction from additional requested work;
- prepare an accurate invoice immediately after approval;
- reduce administrative follow-up; and
- preserve a shared record if memories or expectations later diverge.

The homeowner wants to:

- understand what was promised for the stage;
- see credible evidence of completion;
- know what decision is being requested;
- explain concerns clearly;
- understand the billing consequence of approval; and
- retain a simple record without adopting contractor software.

## Adoption trigger

A provider is most likely to seek Stagenum after experiencing one or more of:

- a milestone invoice delayed by an unclear approval;
- a dispute about whether work was complete;
- a homeowner requesting additional work as though it were included;
- difficulty reconstructing photos and messages for an invoice;
- growth that makes informal follow-up unreliable;
- a desire to present a more professional client experience; or
- dissatisfaction with moving information among documentation, messaging, and
  invoicing tools.

## Positioning hypothesis

### Short form

> Stagenum helps residential contractors document each project stage, get a
> clear homeowner decision, and turn approved work into an invoice draft.

### Differentiation

Stagenum is not positioned as a full construction-management or accounting
suite. Its initial differentiation is the traceable handoff from stage evidence
to client decision to billing readiness.

## Customer interview profile

Initial discovery should include people who:

- currently own or manage a residential contracting or remodeling business;
- have completed at least one multi-stage homeowner project recently;
- personally participate in progress documentation, client approval, invoicing,
  or payment follow-up;
- use milestone or progress billing at least some of the time; and
- can walk through a specific recent project rather than only discuss general
  preferences.

Recruit a varied sample across business maturity, project type, current software,
and administrative support. Avoid recruiting only technically enthusiastic
providers, because ease of adoption is part of the hypothesis.

## Interview topics

Ask about actual recent behavior before showing the proposed product:

1. Walk me through the last project you billed in stages.
2. How were stages and payment expectations established?
3. What did you capture while the work was underway, and where was it stored?
4. How did the homeowner know a stage was ready for review?
5. What counted as approval in practice?
6. Tell me about the last time a client wanted something changed.
7. How did you decide whether it was included or additional scope?
8. When and how was the invoice created?
9. Where did the process slow down or require repeated follow-up?
10. What tools were involved, and which record did you trust afterward?

Avoid asking whether the participant “likes” Stagenum until their existing
behavior and pain are understood.

## Validation signals

Evidence supporting this launch segment would include:

- repeated examples of approval and billing context spread across tools;
- meaningful delays between stage completion, client decision, and invoicing;
- providers already taking photos or maintaining evidence for client confidence;
- recurring confusion between requested corrections and expanded scope;
- willingness to invite a real homeowner into a lightweight review flow;
- willingness to pilot Stagenum on an upcoming project; and
- willingness to pay that is connected to a demonstrated business outcome.

## Disconfirming signals

Reconsider the segment or product approach if discovery shows that:

- explicit stage approval is rarely needed or valued;
- payment delay is driven mostly by financing rather than workflow ambiguity;
- homeowners consistently refuse digital review;
- providers will not define stages or acceptance criteria;
- existing tools already solve the complete workflow satisfactorily;
- evidence capture creates more work than value;
- specialized construction administration is required before the core workflow
  is useful; or
- providers express interest but will not test the product on real work.

## Initial validation threshold

Before treating the segment as validated, Stagenum should:

- interview multiple qualified providers across more than one residential project
  type;
- observe the same core problem in several recent, concrete workflows;
- identify providers willing to share sanitized examples of their current
  process;
- recruit a small set of design partners willing to test a prototype; and
- confirm that at least some design partners will pilot the workflow on a real
  project.

The team should record interview evidence and decide numeric thresholds before
recruitment begins rather than selecting them afterward to fit the results.
The recruitment method, interview guide, evidence scorecard, and predefined
thresholds are documented in the [Contractor Interview
Plan](../research/contractor-interview-plan.md).

## Expansion path

If the shared workflow is validated, likely adjacent segments include:

1. specialty residential trades and installers;
2. other field-based independent service providers with milestone billing;
3. creative freelancers with staged deliverables and revision cycles; and
4. consultants and remote professional-services businesses.

Expansion should reuse the core stage, evidence, Change Request, approval, and
invoice concepts while adapting language, templates, and integrations to the new
segment.

## Open research questions

1. Which residential project types experience the sharpest approval-to-payment
   delay?
2. Who performs the administrative workflow in businesses with more than one
   employee?
3. What currently counts as acceptable evidence to homeowners?
4. How formal are milestone definitions before work begins?
5. Which existing tool owns invoicing, and should Stagenum complement or replace
   it initially?
6. What event makes the provider willing to invite a homeowner into a new tool?
7. How much contractor-specific language is necessary for the product to feel
   credible?
8. What measurable outcome would make the provider pay for Stagenum?
